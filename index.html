Read this before believing it

Half and double. A box search will often land on twice the true period, or half of it. Fold at the answer and look: if the two halves of the plot both show a dip, halve the period.

Detection strength is how many standard deviations the peak stands above the rest of the periodogram. Below about 7 the detection is not trustworthy on its own.

Duration comes out short. A box has vertical sides and a real transit does not, so the fitted width lands inside the true first to fourth contact time by roughly the ingress duration.

b and a/Rstar trade against each other. A large planet crossing the edge makes nearly the same dip as a smaller one crossing the middle. Only the shape of the shoulders separates them, so those two land within about twenty per cent while the radius ratio itself comes out to a few per cent.

Limb darkening is yours to set. u1 and u2 depend on the star and on the wavelength observed. The defaults suit the star loaded. Wrong coefficients bias the radius.

Depth alone assumes the crossing is central. Depth gives Rp/Rs only for a planet crossing the middle of the disc, and only with no limb darkening. Off centre or a bright limb both make the real planet larger than this says.
